## What this measures

Stand at the start and hold still for about 30 seconds. Walk the alignment. Stand at the end and hold still again. The straight line is the distance between those two marks. The walked path is the length of the track recorded while you walk. Use the walked path when the alignment bends. The straight line is shorter whenever the line bends.

The road does not need to exist on a map. The track is not snapped to a road.

## How to measure

Use a phone outdoors, with this page open and the screen on. A phone stops location updates when the page is in the background.

Stand on the start and tap Mark start. Hold still until the fixes agree and the button is ready, then tap it again. Walk the alignment. At the end, hold still until Mark end is ready, then tap it.

A mark uses the fixes from the last 30 seconds. It locks when at least 15 of them, spread over at least 20 seconds, all sit within 3.5 meters of their average. Two marks like that combine to 5 meters or less, and only then does the page show a distance. A fix worse than 20 meters is ignored. A fix that is still moving is ignored. While you walk, a point is kept only after you move at least 5 meters, and only when that step is no faster than a walk.

## How to read the result

Both lengths are whole meters. The ± figure is how tightly the phone repeated each mark. It is never shown above 5 meters. It is not a survey tolerance. A jumping fix under trees or against a wall will not lock. A steady fix beside a building can still be shifted by more than 5 meters. The walked length can still be long if the fix wanders, or short if a bend was sampled sparsely. The plot is north-up and uses only the positions already on this phone. Download the record if you need the coordinates. The file stays on this device.

## What this does not do

Averaging a still removes jitter. It does not move a reflected signal onto the true spot. Do not use it as the only figure on an official requirement.
